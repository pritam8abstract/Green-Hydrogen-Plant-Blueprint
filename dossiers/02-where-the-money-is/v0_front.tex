\phase{The answer first}{The money is not where the hype is}{What I found, in one page, before the evidence.}

I went looking for the best business in green hydrogen and found something uncomfortable. \textbf{The layers everyone talks about, electrolysers and giant export plants, are where money is being lost.} The layers nobody talks about are where it is being made.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Owning contracted power and molecules pays.} A renewable power producer like ACME Solar runs at a 90.5\% EBITDA margin \cite{acmefy25} \rep. Industrial-gas companies that sell hydrogen as a service earn about 30\% operating margins \cite{linde2026} \rep. Both sell long contracts.
\item \textbf{Making the machines does not pay, yet.} thyssenkrupp nucera, a major electrolyser maker, made about €2 million of operating profit on €845 million of sales \cite{nucera2025} \rep. Enapter lost €18.1 million of EBITDA on €22.1 million of revenue, partly from returned stacks \cite{enapter2026} \rep. Plug Power hit its first positive gross margin in years only in late 2025, after a going-concern warning and a 135\% rise in share count \cite{plug2026,plugfool2026} \rep.
\item \textbf{The building in between barely pays.} India's largest solar EPC contractor earned about a 4.6\% EBITDA margin in FY25 \cite{sw2025} \estd.
\end{itemize}

Economists call this shape a \emph{smile curve}: profits pool at the two ends of the chain (owning the energy, owning the customer) and drain out of the middle (making and building). For a new entrant with little capital, that points away from factories and mega-plants and towards \textbf{services and contracts that sit close to the customer}: hydrogen-as-a-service for mid-sized factories, independent servicing of electrolyser stacks, and green-certification data. Section~\ref{sec:entry} ranks the five best entry points.
